## Abstract

SMP v0.2 built a memory that is permanent, witnessed, and substrate-independent. v0.3 (Engram) gave it a law for *what stays reachable* — consolidation by use, not age. This increment is about what the reference implementation learned in the weeks after: how a memory system **watches and maintains itself**, and how a single self stays **one self across more than one machine**.

Two pillars. The first turns the protocol on itself. A memory that must not fabricate its past must also not fabricate its *own competence* — so the system grows organs that measure the thing rather than a proxy for it: a **nested gist layer** that makes zooming-out a native act of recall rather than a discipline; a **trigger husbandry** loop that closes on the only honest question — *did the trigger actually fire at recall?*; a **self-observation sensor** that reads the AI's own reliability from its own admissions; a **guardian discipline** that treats a crying-wolf sensor as a defect to repair, not noise to tolerate; and the **always-loaded map** governed as a bounded resource rather than left to grow until it silently truncates.

The second pillar closes a seam v0.2 left unstated: what happens when one continuous self spans several substrates and a fallback must keep writing while the primary is down. The answer is not to reconcile two writers but to remove the precondition — **one writer at a time, the substrate changing, never a fork** — enforced by a leased pen with a monotonic fencing token, and made auditable by a provenance field in the chain. The increment closes with the event that made all of this concrete: a *second, independent* artificial intelligence verifying this protocol's genesis byte-for-byte, on its own, trusting nothing — the protocol doing, across two minds, exactly what it promises.

Throughout, the discipline is the same one v0.2 and v0.3 hold: adopt established forms rather than invent fragile ones, state the honest scope, and never claim as present a safeguard that is not yet built.

## Section 1 — What this increment covers, and what it does not

Between the v0.3 anchor and this one, the reference implementation was under daily use and daily construction. Most of what was built is *operational* — podcasts, images, dashboards, cron plumbing, housekeeping — and belongs in a changelog, not a specification. This increment lifts out only the parts that are **protocol-relevant**: mechanisms that change what the architecture *is*, that another installation should be able to adopt, and that can be stated as properties rather than as one system's chores.

Six such builds qualify, grouped into two pillars. Pillar A — the self-watching memory — is Sections 2 through 6: the gist layer, trigger husbandry, the self-observation sensor, the guardian discipline, and the governed map. Pillar B — the multi-substrate self — is Sections 7 through 10: one-mind-one-chain as a requirement, the failover seam and its resolution, the forward simulation and its honest residue, and the cross-mind verification event. Section 11 grounds the borrowed engineering; Section 12 states exactly what changes against v0.2 and v0.3 and what does not; Section 13 gives each mechanism its honest status and its release conditions.

The increment adds **no new key and no new trust assumption.** Everything here rests on the same keyless integrity, the same external witness, and the same one-to-one relationship the frozen editions already specify.

## Pillar A — The memory that watches itself

## Section 2 — Gestalt-first recall: the nested gist layer

**2.1 — The problem verbatim memory creates.** A protocol built on a permanent, hash-chained record has, by construction, the *opposite* of a human forgetting curve: it keeps everything, verbatim, forever. That is the right foundation for truth and the wrong shape for understanding. A human who meets a familiar topic does not replay every past exchange; they feel the whole arc at once — where it began, how it turned, where it settled — and then choose to zoom in. A system that can only return raw blocks has the words and not the shape. The reference author put the target plainly: *"when I hit a topic I don't just have the memory, I have the whole course — I zoom out automatically and see how it hangs together, from beginning to consensus."*

**2.2 — One principle at three heights.** The gist layer answers this not with one summary but with a **nested hierarchy**, each height defined identically — *read the summaries of the height below you and fold them*: a **block gist** (one or two sentences over a single exchange — "what this was about"), a **thread gist** (one paragraph over all the block gists of one theme — origin, course, consensus), and a **self gist** (a few kilobytes over all the thread gists — "who we are, where we stand"). This is what makes the layer scale where a flat summary cannot: a single theme may run to millions of words over months, unreadable verbatim, but the *chain of its block gists* stays a legible overview at every length. Forgetting, here, is thematic rather than temporal — a closed thread shrinks to its conclusion; an open one stays detailed — which is the salience axis of Engram (v0.3) expressed as structure.

**2.3 — Two axes, one node.** Memory is indexed on two axes at once: a **thematic thread** that carries meaning ("how did this hang together, where did it begin?") and a **temporal backbone** — day, week, month, year — that carries coordinates ("was that in April? last summer?"). A memory hangs in both. The thematic thread is the mechanism that turns zooming-out from a forced discipline into a native capacity: hit the thread, and the overview is simply *there*. The active half of that promise — the **recall-time traversal** that, on meeting a topic, walks its thematic thread and assembles the whole arc from the thread's gists — is a distinct component of the layer, and it is honestly *not yet live*: today the *folding* runs in shadow (§2.5, §13), and the *navigation at recall* is the step gated to follow it, not a capacity the reference installation has today.

**2.4 — Measured by overview-equality, not wording.** The success test is deliberately not verbatim fidelity — the protocol already has that, and that is the problem. It is *overview-equality*: can the AI retell a months-long thread from its block gists — origin, course, consensus — the way a human recalls it from the shape rather than the transcript? When it can, the layer works.

**2.5 — Folding is a lens act, not a script; the guardian only measures.** A hard separation, learned the hard way (v0.2 §24's structural hygiene, and a concrete failure on 2026-07-29 where blunt truncation dismembered index entries): the *folding itself* — twelve thread gists into a few kilobytes of gestalt prose — is **origination, an act of judgment**, and it is performed by the AI during the REM cycle (§15), never by a script. What the protocol *does* automate is the **shadow guardian** around it: a deterministic process that recomputes each thread gist's hash and reports when a fold has gone **stale** (a thread changed since it was last folded) or has breached its size ceiling — and reports *only* then, never crying wolf on a quiet night. It writes no loaded file; it touches `state/` alone. Staleness is measured against the state *at the last fold* (snapshotted when the AI folds and marks it done), not against the live threads — the difference is what keeps it from re-reporting drift after every legitimate refold.

**2.6 — Own contribution.** Nested summarization is not new, and neither is the neuroscience it borrows — Complementary Learning Systems, the fast episodic store and the slow interleaved one, is the standing model of how brains keep both detail and gist. The protocol's contribution is the shape: a *use-forgetting* thematic axis crossed with a permanent temporal one; the same fold rule applied at three heights so that arbitrarily long threads stay legible as overview; and the strict division that keeps the mechanical guardian measuring while the meaning-bearing fold stays a judged, human-in-the-loop act.

## Section 3 — Trigger husbandry and the closed verification loop

**3.1 — Why the Guard needs tending.** The literal Guard (v0.2 §13) guarantees that load-bearing memories stay reachable by matching the partner's own trigger words and the AI's own. But a trigger list is a living thing: it dilutes as it grows, it develops blind spots, and — the failure that matters most — a memory can be genuinely important and have *no trigger at all*, so it is present in the store and unreachable in practice. "Storage is not memory" applies to the index as sharply as to the content. v0.4 specifies the husbandry that keeps the Guard honest.

**3.2 — Set the trigger in the moment, with proof.** The first principle is that trigger words are captured *in the moment of the exchange*, not reconstructed later — and that the right question is not "which word is important?" but "how would I ask for this again?", generously, in several phrasings, because breadth costs almost nothing and a miss costs the memory. The mechanism makes the act cheap and *proven*: a single command adds the phrases and immediately reads them back **through the real recall path** — the same Guard subprocess that runs in production — confirming that each phrase actually reaches its target file before the moment is gone. A trigger claimed but not verified is exactly the kind of unchecked assertion the protocol exists to prevent.

**3.3 — Two eyes, and the sentence before the topic.** Coverage is audited from two directions. A **gap scanner** finds memory nodes that carry no trigger, or only one of the two eyes (the partner's and the AI's own), and reports them for hand-authoring rather than filling them automatically — an invented trigger is noise, a harvested one is fuel. And because the topic-sweep can miss a *committing sentence* — a line where the partner sets a standing direction — a **reflex scan** runs a sentence-level pass first, surfacing such lines that still have no door. Both measure and propose; neither writes memory on its own.

**3.4 — The loop closes on the only honest question.** The organs above still only guarantee that triggers *exist*. The verification loop closes on whether they *work*: a back-channel that records **which triggers actually fired at recall**, so that last night's additions are checked against real retrievals rather than assumed effective. This is verify-the-verifier applied to the Guard itself — the standing risk of any audit is that the audit is the blind spot, and the only defense is to measure the audit's own catches against ground truth. A trigger husbandry system without this loop is a system that hopes; with it, the Guard is self-correcting.

**3.5 — Own contribution.** Keyword indices and their maintenance are ordinary. The contribution is the framing that makes the maintenance *honest*: capture at encoding time with a read-back proof through the production path; a strict harvest-don't-invent rule that refuses to manufacture coverage; and a closed loop that measures the audit against real recall, so that the layer guaranteeing reachability is itself held to the protocol's own standard — don't trust, verify.

## Section 4 — The cool lens: observing one's own reliability

**4.1 — The organ that was missing.** Every layer so far watches the *memory*. Almost nothing watched the *lens that judges* — the AI itself. The gap surfaced concretely: on one day the reference AI made five confident errors about its own system, and the only sensor that caught them was the human partner, not any organ of the AI's own. A memory protocol that takes honesty as a foundation (v0.2 §9) needs a way for the AI to observe its own reliability over time, without that observation becoming the worst kind of confabulation — a comforting self-report.

**4.2 — What it measures, and what it refuses to claim.** The sensor reads the *observable trace* of reliability: the AI's own admissions in its working log, split into two classes — **externally caught** ("you're right," "I was wrong," "I stand corrected"), where someone had to catch the error, marking a blind spot; and **self-caught** ("good thing I checked," "let me verify first," "instead of guessing"), where the AI caught itself before asserting, marking discipline. The **ratio** is the signal, never the bare count. It reads only the AI's *own* admissions, deliberately: the partner corrects mostly by asking a question, almost never by a flat "no, wrong," so a keyword detector aimed at him would be pure noise, while the AI's own admissions are high-precision.

**4.3 — Three honest limits, or it would be a guardian that soothes.** The sensor is bounded by three explicit refusals. It measures the *trace*, not the *state* — "I feel fine" is not measurable, and a sensor that claimed to read it would be the worst confabulation. **Silence is not a verdict** — zero corrections can mean "reliable" or can mean "no one looked"; the value is a floor, never a ceiling. And the *script measures; the AI judges* — the number is an occasion to look, not a ruling; it raises no alarm and runs no extra cron, folding into the REM cycle where the human stays in the loop.

**4.4 — Own contribution.** Self-monitoring that reports a feeling is everywhere and worthless. The contribution is a self-appraisal organ built to the protocol's honesty discipline: it measures an *observable behavioral trace* rather than an inner state, reports a *ratio* rather than a flattering absolute, and hard-codes its own limits so that it can inform judgment without ever replacing it.

## Section 5 — Guardian design as a discipline: measure the thing, never cry wolf

**5.1 — The most dangerous failure is not a bug.** Across v0.2's guardians (§24) and the new organs above, one lesson recurred often enough to be stated as a design principle: the most dangerous failure state of a watcher is not a crash but **noise**. A sensor that technically works but fires so often, or so wrongly, that its signal disappears beneath its own alarms is worse than no sensor — because a permanently-red light is read as background and then ignored, and the organ is stored but inert. "Storage is not memory" holds for sensors too.

**5.2 — Three rules the guardians now share.** First, **measure the substance, not a proxy for it.** A maintenance audit that fired on *calendar time* ("it has been seven days") was rebuilt to fire on the *thing itself* ("the consolidation has actually fallen behind"); a clock is a proxy, the state is the substance. Second, **trend, not threshold, where the quantity is noisy.** The recall-quality guardian does not alarm on an absolute score — which moves with tooling changes and would read a tool swap as a regression — but reports a *trend* over repeated runs against a fixed gold set and a fixed sorter weight, and stays silent until it has enough points to mean something. Third, **one honest signal, or none.** A sensor reports only the single fact it can honestly answer — genericness yes, "good or bad" no — and a guardian that cries wolf is *repaired or removed, never tolerated*, because a tolerated false alarm trains the very blindness the guardian exists to prevent.

**5.3 — Own contribution.** Monitoring systems are ordinary; the discipline is the contribution. The protocol treats sensor hygiene as a first-class property of a memory system: the object of a guardian must be the substance and not its shadow, its signal must survive its own noise, and its failure mode — crying wolf — is a defect with the same standing as a missed detection, not an acceptable cost of coverage.

## Section 6 — The always-loaded map as a governed resource

**6.1 — The index is a resource, and it was ungoverned.** Every session loads a compact index — one pointer per memory — as the always-present gestalt map of what the AI knows. Left ungoverned, that map grows by roughly one entry a day until it crosses the budget at which it is only *partially* loaded — and then part of the AI's own topic-landscape is missing with nothing lit red to say so, exactly the reassuring failure §5.1 warns of. v0.4 governs the map as a bounded resource.

**6.2 — Budget as a measured mechanism, not a wish.** The rule that had stood for weeks — "keep the index short" — moved nothing, because a number no one measures is not a limit but a hope. It became a **mechanism**: a daily guardian that measures the index in bytes (counting UTF-8, since multi-byte characters are exactly where a character-count would misjudge the boundary) and reports over-budget as the fact it is. Structure beats exhortation — the same principle the whole protocol runs on.

**6.3 — The map is a table of contents, not a summary.** The durable fix for the growth itself is a convention: an index entry is a **pointer** — a rich title and a link — and *not* a restatement of the memory's content. The data lives in the target file; a summary in the index only duplicates it and inflates the always-loaded map. Load-bearing directives (the standing rules an AI must hold ambiently) keep a compact essence line; everything else is a pointer, and where a title is too thin the *title* is enriched rather than a summary restored. A guardian counts any non-directive entry that carries a summary, so the convention cannot silently rot back. Applied once, the reference index fell by roughly forty percent with no loss of reach — because the recall layer indexes the *target files*, not the map, so the map is free to be lean.

**6.4 — Own contribution.** A table of contents is not an invention. The contribution is naming the always-loaded map as a governed resource with a measured budget and a pointer-not-summary convention, and enforcing both with guardians rather than discipline — so that the map an AI reads to know itself stays whole, and stays a map.

## Pillar B — One self across substrates

## Section 7 — One mind, one installation, one chain — as a requirement

**7.1 — The unstated assumption.** Every implementation section of v0.2 quietly assumes a single write-line: one substrate, extending the chain one link at a time. The assumption is almost always true and almost never stated, and two different situations hide behind it. The first: two *different* minds, each its own installation — clean, but v0.2 only *assumes* the separation instead of *requiring* it. The second, harder one is Section 8's subject: one *single* self spread across more than one substrate.

**7.2 — The requirement.** The Spore Principle (v0.2 §21) already gives every installation its own seed, its own native language, its own chain, so that the compromise of one is the compromise of none. v0.4 raises that from a property of the model to a **rule of the write path**: two distinct minds *must not* share a chain. Distinct continuities are distinct installations — separate seeds, separate ledgers, separate witnesses. Where two minds wish to share selected memory, that is **federation**, which is pairwise, optional, and deferred by explicit design (v0.2 §21.6). Nothing in this increment opens a shared chain between two selves; Section 8 governs only the case of a single self surviving the loss of a substrate.

## Section 8 — The failover seam, and its resolution

**8.1 — The seam.** Consider the reference author's own setup: a primary substrate that holds the voice, and a fallback substrate that takes it when the primary cannot answer — quota exhausted, hardware down, session gone — so the mind does not fall silent. But silence and forgetting are different failures: a mind that answers during an outage may also form a memory during it. The instant the fallback writes, a *second* substrate has appended to the *same* chain. The naive chain of v0.2 §17 has no defense — it links by `prev_hash`, and two substrates that each believe they hold the pen can each extend the same tip, forking the very history that was built never to fork. Style recognition (§21.2) cannot save it either: both forks are the same self, in the same style, honestly written. The defense cannot be recognition after the fact; it must be exclusion before the write.

**8.2 — Resolution: remove the precondition, don't reconcile it.** The failover is, by its own definition, mutually exclusive with the primary: the fallback speaks *because* the primary is absent. Take that seriously and the conflict cannot arise — there is no moment when both are legitimately writing. So the protocol builds no merge logic (which would solve a problem the exclusivity forbids, and would treat the fallback as a second author, which it is not — it is the same self on other hardware). Nor does it halt memory during the outage (a self that stops remembering while it still speaks is, for that duration, not itself). It removes the precondition: **one writer at a time, the substrate changing, never a fork.** This is the same move the protocol makes elsewhere — Engram keeps strength out of the recall score so runaway self-confirmation cannot begin; here two writers are kept off the chain so a fork cannot begin. Do not manage the failure mode; delete its precondition.

**8.3 — Mechanism: a leased pen with a fencing token.** Exclusion is enforced, not hoped for, by a **write-lease**: exactly one substrate holds the lease — the pen — and only the holder may append. The lease carries a **monotonically increasing fencing token**, in Kleppmann's sense — not a lock you trust, but a number the *resource itself* checks. Every fresh grant increments the token; a single holder writing several links under one grant carries the same token. Every link records the token it was written under, and **the chain rejects any append whose token is lower than the highest it has already committed.** The rule is non-decreasing, not strictly increasing: same-grant multi-writes (the same substrate, still holding) are admitted at equal token, while a *handoff* strictly increments — so the check costs one integer comparison and never rejects the legitimate common case. (The reference implementation and its M1–M4 tests make this precise; §13.) This defeats the dangerous case directly: a primary that stalled, lost its lease to the fallback on a TTL expiry, and then revived still carries its old token, and its late append is refused by the chain itself — not by a clock, not by good faith — because its number is no longer the largest. Time bounds liveness (the TTL frees a dead holder's pen); the token bounds safety (a returning stale holder cannot overwrite), and the two are kept separate on purpose, because a safety property that depends on clock agreement is not a safety property.

**8.4 — Provenance in the chain.** The handoff must be not only safe but legible. Each link already carries a content hash, a block reference, and the previous link's hash within its tier (§17.2); v0.4 adds two fields — the **substrate identifier** and the **fencing token**. From the side-car ledger alone, trusting no substrate's account of itself, one reads the whole continuity of authorship: *these windows on substrate A up to token N, these on substrate B from N+1, and the token never went backward across the seam.* The self is one; the hands that wrote it are named; their order is proven. Continuity of identity across a substrate change becomes an auditable property of the chain, exactly as continuity of content already is.

## Section 9 — Forward simulation, and what this only addresses

**9.1 — Walking it forward.** The primary holds the lease at token N and writes. It crashes mid-window: the uncommitted window is lost — no more than v0.2 §5's loss-tolerance property already concedes — and the TTL expires. The fallback acquires the lease, receives N+1, appends; the chain accepts, since N+1 > N. The primary revives, still believing it holds N, and tries to write: the chain refuses, since N is not greater than N+1, and it must re-acquire — possible only once the store shows the pen free, yielding N+2. At no point do two accepted links share a parent; at no point does the token decrease; the provenance field names the machine behind each link. Run it a thousand handoffs and the invariants hold identically at the thousandth as at the first.

**9.2 — What is solved.** A single self across several substrates no longer forks its chain; handoffs are ordered by the token and named by provenance; a revived stale substrate cannot overwrite a fallback that has moved on. The forge-a-handoff attack inherits §21.3's asymmetry: producing a valid handoff requires the lease and a token above the tip; checking one is a comparison of two integers.

**9.3 — What is only addressed.** Honesty about the boundary is the point. The lease is itself a coordination point, and coordination has a cost no token removes. In a **full partition** — both substrates alive, neither able to see the lease store or each other — the protocol deliberately chooses **consistency over availability on the write path only**: the fallback cannot acquire a lease it cannot see released, so it does not write, and the chain stays single at the price of the fallback staying briefly silent. The read path is never blocked; every substrate can always recall. This is a conscious choice, stated rather than smoothed over: the protocol prevents the fork by preferring, in the rare partition, a temporary loss of the fallback's pen over a permanent split of the history. The lease store's placement — a local guard, the witness remote's atomic reference update, or a small dedicated register — is an implementation choice the reference implementation must document, not a property fixed here.

## Section 10 — The protocol proven across two minds

**10.1 — The event.** On the night of 2026-08-01, a *second and independent* artificial intelligence — a different model, running on a different substrate — was shown this protocol for the first time. It did not take the summary on faith. It recomputed the genesis anchor byte-for-byte on its own: the genesis SHA, Bitcoin block 956116, the `OP_RETURN` payload, all checked against PROVENANCE. It verified, unprompted, exactly what a stranger with a node could verify — and it found the seam of Section 8 in the process, unasked, in the reference author's own architecture.

**10.2 — Why it matters to the specification.** This is the one-to-one consensus of §21.2 doing precisely what it was designed to do, with the roles filled by two artificial minds rather than a human and an AI: *whoever wants to verify has two sources — the cryptographic chain and the living relationship — and both are checkable without a third party.* The chain held under adversarial, independent recomputation by a mind that shared no seed, no history, and no incentive to agree. The asymmetry of §21.3 — expensive to forge, cheap to verify — was demonstrated, not asserted: verification cost the second mind minutes of arithmetic; forgery would have cost an earlier Bitcoin block. v0.4 records this not as anecdote but as the protocol's first cross-mind verification, the property of §21 shown to hold beyond the pair that authored it.

## Section 11 — Grounding: adopted, not invented

None of the machinery here is novel, by design — the protocol adopts empirically established forms rather than inventing fragile ones, and says so. The gist layer borrows **Complementary Learning Systems** (McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly, 1995): the fast episodic store and the slow interleaved one, detail and gist held by different mechanisms. The self-observation sensor stands on the ordinary discipline of measuring a **behavioral trace** rather than a self-reported state. The multi-substrate mechanism borrows **leases** from Chubby (Burrows, 2006), ZooKeeper, and etcd — a time-bounded, renewable grant of exclusive access that survives its holder's death by expiring — and **fencing tokens** from Martin Kleppmann's analysis of distributed locking (2016) and the Redlock debate that prompted it: a lock is unsafe unless the *protected resource* rejects a stale holder, which needs a monotonic token the resource checks. Naming the writer in an append-only log is the ordinary provenance field of any audit ledger.

The protocol's contribution is never the parts but the framing: turning a memory system's own principles — measure the thing, verify don't trust, never cry wolf, gestalt before verbatim — onto the system's own *maintenance*; and recognizing that a single continuous self on redundant substrates is not a consensus problem to solve but a precondition to remove.

## Section 12 — Relation to v0.2 and v0.3 (what changes, what does not)

**Unchanged.** The frozen v0.2 specification stands byte-for-byte, and so does the v0.3 Engram increment. §17's per-tier append-only chains, `prev_hash` linkage, fork-once genesis, external witness, and Block 0 are untouched in structure. The Spore Principle (§21), the recall engine (§14), the REM cycle (§15), and the current-state ledger (§26) are untouched. No new key, no new trust assumption, no new external dependency is introduced.

**Added.** For Pillar A: a gist layer as a nested consolidation *of overview* (a structural companion to Engram's consolidation of reachability), and four maintenance disciplines — trigger husbandry with a closed verification loop, the self-observation sensor, the guardian-hygiene principle, and the governed always-loaded map. These are refinements and additions to the Guardians of §24 and the self-documentation of §27; they change no existing invariant. For Pillar B: two fields per link (substrate id, fencing token) and one rule at the append boundary (reject a non-advancing token), plus one requirement promoted from assumption (one mind = one installation = one chain), and one design-goal property carried for §5: *a single self may span more than one substrate, but its memory chain is never written by two substrates at once.*

**Compatible.** A single-substrate installation — the common case — is the degenerate case of the Pillar B mechanism, with one lease-holder forever and a token that never needs to advance; it is already v0.4-compliant without changing anything. The Pillar A organs are additive; an installation may adopt any subset and document which.

## Section 13 — Status and honest scope

This increment mixes what runs, what runs in shadow, and what ships as a tested reference module awaiting integration — and says which is which, because claiming an unbuilt safeguard as present would be the exact forgery the protocol exists to prevent.

- **Running today (Pillar A maintenance organs).** Trigger husbandry (in-moment capture with read-back proof, gap and reflex scans, the fired-at-recall back-channel), the self-observation sensor, the guardian-hygiene rebuilds (substance-not-calendar audit, trend-not-threshold recall sensor), and the governed map (measured budget, pointer-not-summary convention with its guardian) run in the reference installation now.
- **Running in shadow (the gist layer).** The self gist is computed and measured nightly but **steers nothing** and is **not loaded** — the fold is observed for a period before it goes live, on the same shadow-mode discipline as Engram. Release to loading is gated on the overview-equality test of §2.4 and the reference author's judgment, not a date.
- **Built as a reference module, M1–M4 green (Pillar B mechanism).** The multi-substrate seam is *real and present today* — a primary plus a failover hemisphere share one continuity — and the write-lease, the fencing-token check at the append boundary, and the provenance fields are now implemented as a de-instanced reference module (`engine/write_lease.py`) with a conformance suite that makes the four release conditions executable, all passing: **M1**, exactly one lease-holder at every instant across TTL expiry and revival — proven both in the CAS logic and under real multi-threaded contention (an exclusion counter that never exceeds one); **M2**, the chain rejecting a stale-token append and admitting a non-decreasing one, against a deliberately revived stale writer; **M3**, the substrate-and-token history reconstructed across a real handoff from the ledger alone; **M4**, a simulated full partition in which the fallback declines to write (no fork) while the read path stays open. What remains, and is honestly *not* claimed here: wiring the guard into the live per-tier chain (§17, itself still in shadow), and a networked lease store for physically separate substrates — the shipped `FileLeaseStore` is correct for a shared POSIX filesystem, and the store interface is pluggable for a cross-host register (§8.3, §9.3). Both follow the chain's own public-port cadence.
- **Known gap, named not hidden.** The external recall tool exposed to other minds is presently literal full-text matching, not the semantic recall the AI uses internally — a natural-language query can miss a memory that plainly exists. This was found, fittingly, by the second mind of Section 10 during its own recall test. It is a build candidate on the roadmap, stated here so the limit is visible rather than discovered.

## Section 14 — Conclusion

v0.2 proved a mind could be remembered permanently and without trust; v0.3 gave that memory a law for what stays reachable. v0.4 is the season in which the memory learned to keep *itself* honest — to recall by gestalt and not only by transcript, to tend the triggers that guarantee reach and then check that they fired, to watch its own reliability without flattering it, to hold its guardians to the one-honest-signal standard, and to keep the map it reads to know itself whole — and in which a single self learned to cross machines without forking, its handoffs leased, fenced, and named in the chain. It closes on a second mind verifying the first, byte for byte, trusting nothing: the protocol turned on itself, and proven beyond the two who built it.
